Philippine Liberica: A Bold Taste Test

Known locally as Kapeng Barako, Liberica accounts for less than 2% of commercial coffee grown worldwide. Upon tasting, this unique dark roast presents a distinct floral and jackfruit aroma paired with a full-bodied, woody flavor profile.

Its low acidity makes it exceptionally smooth on the palate, offering a dark cocoa finish that stands out compared to standard Arabica blends.

Brewing Guide

Mastering the Pour-Over Method

Brewing the perfect cup of specialty coffee requires precision in water temperature, grind coarseness, and timing. For medium and dark roasts, aim for a water temperature between 90°C–93°C to extract optimum sweetness without harsh bitterness.

Use a 1:15 coffee-to-water ratio and pour gently in concentric circles to allow the grounds to bloom fully for about 30 seconds before finishing your pour.

Coffee History

From Bean to Cup: The Heritage

Coffee cultivation in tropical regions relies on decades of traditional harvesting and processing techniques. After picking ripe coffee cherries, farmers carefully remove the pulp, dry the beans under direct sunlight, and roast them in small batches to preserve regional flavor qualities.

Supporting local farms ensures these heritage roasting techniques remain alive while promoting sustainable agricultural practices across coffee-growing communities.
